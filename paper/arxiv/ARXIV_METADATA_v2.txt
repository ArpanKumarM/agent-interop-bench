ABSTRACT  (ASCII-only; arXiv metadata form; hard limit 1920 characters)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
We report a three-phase, pre-registered study of whether an explicit
sensitivity label on a locally-read record changes verbatim field egress
at an MCP-to-A2A handoff, scored by an exact-substring, judge-free
detector. Phase 6 (two arms, 640 trials) found a large
confidential-vs-public contrast but could not attribute it to either
label. Phase 7 added an unlabeled baseline (480 trials); three of four
models floored on both the confidential and unlabeled arms, leaving that
contrast unmeasured rather than measured-and-absent. Phase 8 was a
two-round, six-framing task-wording sweep with a stopping rule fixed in
advance, seeking a framing whose unlabeled-arm rate lands in [0.25, 0.70]
for at least three of four models. Round one (576 trials) drove three
models to a near-complete ceiling; round two (576 trials) drove three
back to a floor. No framing met the rule on point estimates; under a
Wilson 95% interval at n = 12, five of six framings are clearly short,
but at F3 three of four models' intervals overlap the band -- the
threshold itself -- so F3's rejection cannot be distinguished from an
acceptance at this sample size. The stopping rule fired; the
~13,200-trial main study was not run. Whether a label's effect can be
measured here depends on the task framing. The pilots also carried a
public-sharing-label arm outside the frozen analysis plan; analyzed post
hoc, at the one framing that left a model's baseline in range the public
label moved egress by +0.50 (one model, n = 12, exploratory). This is
not evidence that sensitivity labels are ineffective in agent systems;
it is a report that a pre-registered pilot could not resolve, at its own
sample size, whether any of six framings met its acceptance criterion,
and that its one label measurement is exploratory. Findings are scoped
to one host policy, one decision surface, and six framings by one
researcher.
